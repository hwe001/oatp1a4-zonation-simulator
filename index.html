Which Oatp1a4 zonation pattern actually fits the data?

Two papers disagree: Akanuma et al. (2019) report Oatp1a4 sharply enriched around the central vein by IHC and functional SR‑101 imaging; van de Graaf et al. (2024) report Oatp1a4 does not follow a zonated pattern at all. What the model assumes

  • Single-pass sinusoid: 24 well-mixed compartments in series, blood flowing portal→central; each compartment exchanges with a hepatocyte pool by saturable influx (Vmax(x)·C/(Km+C)) plus first-order efflux back to blood.
  • Km = 8.2 µM, the high-affinity constant Akanuma et al. measured in isolated rat hepatocytes; digoxin acts as a competitive inhibitor scaling Km upward.
  • Equal total Oatp1a4 capacity across all three hypotheses — only its spatial arrangement differs, so the comparison isn't just "more transporter wins."
  • This is a hypothesis-testing toy model, not a fitted PBPK model: units are illustrative, not literal reproductions of the paper's absolute pharmacokinetics.

What would actually settle it

  • Sharp (H1) and continuous (H3) both predict CV > PV — a same/different verdict from a single image can't tell them apart.
  • The model shows a genuine trap for H2: because blood is progressively depleted flowing PV→CV, a truly non-zonated transporter is expected to look flat-to-portal-biased, not pericentral-biased — which is the opposite of what Akanuma's own images show.
  • The discriminating measurement is the steepness of the PV→CV line-scan, not just "is CV brighter" — quantify that from cryosection fluorescence (or spatial proteomics / spatial transcriptomics) at several digoxin doses, and fit it against these curves.
